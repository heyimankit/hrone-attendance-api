# DECISIONS.md

1. **Indexes.** On `employees`: unique `emp_code` (uniqueness and lookups), `(department, emp_code)` (sorted listing), and `(department, joined_on)` (headcount filtering). On `attendance_logs`: unique `(emp_code, date)` (single daily record constraint and punch-in concurrency), `(emp_code, punch_in)` (fast punch-out search), `(date, emp_code)` (listing sort), and `(date, late_minutes)` (leaderboard). We considered and rejected an all-inclusive covering index on attendance derived fields due to severe write amplification during high-frequency punches.
2. **Punch-in race.** Both requests validate the employee, calculate the IST attendance date, and attempt an atomic `insert_one`. The unique index on `(emp_code, date)` serializes the operation in MongoDB: exactly one succeeds returning HTTP 201, while the competing insert fails with `DuplicateKeyError`, caught and translated into HTTP 409 Conflict.
3. **Ties.** Competition ranking (`$rank` via `$setWindowFields`) assigns tied employees identical ranks and skips subsequent values (1, 2, 2, 4). Filtering `rank <= limit` applies after ranking, so all employees tied at or below the cutoff are returned (e.g. limit 2 returns 3 rows if ranks are 1, 2, 2).
4. **Headcount.** Department summary aggregates from `employees` matching `joined_on <= end_date`, left-joining (`$lookup`) attendance logs. Grouping on `department` counts employee documents (`$sum: 1`), accurately recording headcount even when an employee has zero logs.
5. **100x Data Growth.** At 10 million records, we would shard `attendance_logs` by `(emp_code, date)` and maintain incremental pre-aggregated daily/monthly summaries via MongoDB Change Streams rather than on-demand full collection aggregation scans.
